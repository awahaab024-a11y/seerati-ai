How to Write an ATS-Friendly Resume

Applicant tracking systems parse your resume into fields before a person reads it. Clean structure helps that parsing. No tool can promise a pass, but avoiding common formatting mistakes removes avoidable risk.

  • Single column, no tables, icons or text inside images
  • Keywords from the job description, only where they are true for you
  • Bullets that start with a verb and show a result

What our score means

The percentage is a rough guide from five published criteria: keywords, title match, section structure, contact details and readability. It does not guarantee that any specific ATS will accept your resume.

Common formatting mistakes

  • Putting contact details in the page header or footer
  • Showing experience in tables or side-by-side columns
  • Using icons or images instead of words
  • Using creative headings instead of standard ones
  • Saving the resume as an image inside a PDF

How to use the keyword report

Look at the missing keywords first. If you genuinely have the experience, add it where it fits naturally. If you do not, leave it out, because the goal is an accurate and clear resume, not a higher number. The score helps you find gaps; it is not a prediction of acceptance or rejection.

A quick pre-submit check

Can you select and copy the text in your PDF? Are your contact details at the top of the page body? Does your job title appear near the top? Are your dates consistent? If the answers are yes, your resume is in good shape for most systems, with no promise about any specific one.

FAQ

Do I need exact keywords?

Use the wording of the job where it is accurate for you.

Is a PDF safe?

A text-based PDF is usually fine; avoid scanned images.

Does the score guarantee anything?

No, it is guidance only.
